================================================================================
DEFINITION OF ROBUSTNESS
================================================================================

A strategy is considered ROBUST if it exhibits:

1. Smooth performance degradation
   - No sudden jumps or discontinuities
   - Gradual changes as parameters vary

2. Predictable behavior
   - Performance changes follow expected patterns
   - Monotonic relationships where theory predicts

3. Bounded sensitivity
   - Performance doesn't degrade catastrophically
   - Remains usable across parameter range

4. Stability
   - Similar behavior across different market conditions
   - No regime-dependent failures

We test robustness across:
  - Transaction costs: k ∈ [0.5%, 2.0%] (4× range)
  - Rebalancing frequency: dt ∈ [1/252, 1/21] (12× range)
  - Market volatility: TSLA (σ=0.59) vs AAPL (σ=0.28) (2× range)

BLACK-SCHOLES ROBUSTNESS ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Special case: Black-Scholes shows POOR robustness to k

Why BS fails robustness test:
  - BS ignores transaction costs (assumes k=0)
  - Hedging error variance grows with k
  - No mechanism to compensate

TSLA BS performance:
  k=0.5%: std=1.663
  k=2.0%: std=4.089 (+146%)

This is NOT robust! But is it "broken"?

Analysis:
  ✓ Degradation is smooth (no jumps)
  ✓ Behavior is predictable (monotonic)
  ✓ Follows theory (BS doesn't model TC)

⚠️ BS is robust in sense of "behaves as expected"
✗ But NOT robust in sense of "maintains good performance"

Conclusion: BS exhibits STRUCTURAL non-robustness
  - Fundamental limitation of the model
  - Not a numerical/implementation issue
  - This is WHY Leland (1985) developed modified formula!

================================================================================
THEORETICAL EXPLANATION OF ROBUSTNESS
================================================================================

WHY LELAND STRATEGIES ARE ROBUST TO k:
---------------------------------------

Classical Leland formula: A = (k/σ) × √(8/(π×Δt))

This means modified volatility: σ* = σ√(1 + A)

Hedging error with Leland: err ≈ σ*√(Δt) = σ√(1 + k/σ × √(8/(π×Δt))) × √(Δt)

As k increases:
  - A increases (higher modified volatility)
  - This COMPENSATES for transaction costs
  - Hedging error variance grows slowly

For BS (A=0): err ≈ σ√(Δt) + TC
  - TC grows linearly with k
  - No compensation mechanism
  - Total error grows quickly

This explains why Leland std grows only 10% while BS grows 146%!

WHY ALL STRATEGIES ARE SENSITIVE TO dt:
----------------------------------------

Fundamental limitation: discrete rebalancing

As dt increases:
  - Hedge becomes "stale" between rebalances
  - Stock moves away from target delta
  - Gamma exposure accumulates

Hedging error: err ≈ (1/2) × Γ × (ΔS)² × Δt

Since ΔS ≈ σS√(Δt), we get:
  err ≈ Γ × S² × σ² × Δt^(3/2)

So std grows with dt^(3/4) - superlinear!

This affects ALL strategies equally - it's fundamental.

Only solution: rebalance more frequently (smaller dt)
But this increases transaction costs!

This is the TRADEOFF Leland's formula optimally balances.

   ✗ Performance degrades rapidly with k
