================================================================================
VOLATILITY REGIME EFFECTS ON HEDGING STRATEGIES
================================================================================

Analysis of how market volatility affects the performance and robustness
of different hedging strategies.

Comparison: TSLA (σ=0.5884, high vol) vs AAPL (σ=0.2775, low vol)

================================================================================
KEY FINDING
================================================================================

Market volatility (σ) has PROFOUND impact on:
  1. Absolute hedging performance (error magnitude)
  2. Relative strategy ranking (which strategy wins)
  3. Sensitivity to transaction costs (robustness to k)
  4. Sensitivity to rebalancing frequency (robustness to dt)

However: Classical Leland STILL dominates in both regimes!

================================================================================
1. ABSOLUTE PERFORMANCE SCALING
================================================================================

HOW DOES HEDGING ERROR SCALE WITH VOLATILITY?
----------------------------------------------

Theory predicts: std_error ∝ σ

Test at standard conditions (k=1%, dt=1/252):

TSLA (σ=0.5884):
  Black-Scholes:    std = 2.384
  Classical Leland: std = 1.407
  Optimized:        std = 1.407
  ML:               std = 1.418

AAPL (σ=0.2775):
  Black-Scholes:    std = 2.013
  Classical Leland: std = 0.730
  Optimized:        std = 0.748
  ML:               std = 0.731

Scaling ratios (TSLA/AAPL):
  σ ratio:              0.5884/0.2775 = 2.12
  BS std ratio:         2.384/2.013 = 1.18  ← SUBLINEAR
  Classical std ratio:  1.407/0.730 = 1.93  ← NEARLY LINEAR
  Optimized std ratio:  1.407/0.748 = 1.88  ← NEARLY LINEAR
  ML std ratio:         1.418/0.731 = 1.94  ← NEARLY LINEAR

INTERPRETATION:
---------------

✅ Leland strategies scale CORRECTLY with σ (ratio ≈ 2)
   - Theory: error ∝ σ
   - Empirical: std_TSLA/std_AAPL ≈ σ_TSLA/σ_AAPL
   - This validates the model!

⚠️ Black-Scholes scales POORLY with σ (ratio only 1.18)
   - At high vol: BS badly hurt by transaction costs
   - At low vol: BS less affected (smaller position changes)
   - Non-linear degradation!

Why does this happen?

Black-Scholes ignores k:
  - High σ → large delta changes → high TC → BS fails
  - Low σ → small delta changes → low TC → BS acceptable

Leland compensates for k via A:
  - A = (k/σ) × √(8/πΔt)
  - High σ → small A → less compensation needed
  - Low σ → large A → more compensation needed
  - Scales correctly!

3. TRANSACTION COST SENSITIVITY
================================================================================

HOW DOES VOLATILITY AFFECT ROBUSTNESS TO k?
--------------------------------------------

Classical Leland performance as k varies from 0.5% to 2%:

TSLA (high vol):
  k=0.5%: std = 1.341
  k=2.0%: std = 1.471
  Change: +9.7% (very robust!)

AAPL (low vol):
  k=0.5%: std = 0.666
  k=2.0%: std = 0.842
  Change: +26.4% (less robust)

Ratio of sensitivity: 26.4% / 9.7% = 2.7×

Low volatility stocks are 2.7× MORE SENSITIVE to transaction costs!

WHY?
----

Key parameter: k/σ ratio

TSLA: k/σ = 0.02/0.588 = 0.034  (small)
AAPL: k/σ = 0.02/0.278 = 0.072  (larger)

Leland adjustment: A = (k/σ) × √(8/πΔt)

For AAPL, k/σ is 2.1× larger → A is 2.1× larger
→ Modified volatility more different from true volatility
→ More impact on hedging error

This explains the higher sensitivity!

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION:
----------------------

For low volatility stocks:
  ⚠️ Pay close attention to transaction costs
  ⚠️ Accurate estimation of k is critical
  ⚠️ Hedging error more sensitive to k changes

For high volatility stocks:
  ✓ Transaction costs less critical (relatively)
  ✓ Hedging error dominated by volatility, not k
  ✓ More forgiving to k estimation errors

BLACK-SCHOLES COMPARISON:
-------------------------

BS performance as k varies:

TSLA (high vol):
  k=0.5%: std = 1.663
  k=2.0%: std = 4.089
  Change: +146% (terrible!)

AAPL (low vol):
  k=0.5%: std = 1.149
  k=2.0%: std = 3.851
  Change: +235% (catastrophic!)

BS is MORE sensitive at low vol (235% vs 146%)!

This is opposite to Leland, which handles low vol better.

Reason: BS has no compensation mechanism
  - High vol: large position changes anyway → TC bad
  - Low vol: TC dominates (position changes small but frequent) → TC terrible

4. REBALANCING FREQUENCY SENSITIVITY
================================================================================

HOW DOES VOLATILITY AFFECT ROBUSTNESS TO dt?
---------------------------------------------

Classical Leland performance as dt varies from daily to monthly:

TSLA (high vol):
  dt=1/252: std = 1.519
  dt=1/21:  std = 4.572
  Change: +201% (sensitive)

AAPL (low vol):
  dt=1/252: std = 0.862
  dt=1/21:  std = 2.291
  Change: +166% (less sensitive)

High volatility stocks are MORE SENSITIVE to rebalancing frequency!

WHY?
----

Tracking error between rebalances: ε ∝ Γ × (ΔS)² × dt

Where ΔS ∝ σ√dt

So: ε ∝ σ² × dt^(3/2)

Higher σ → quadratically larger error for same dt

TSLA: σ² = 0.346
AAPL: σ² = 0.077
Ratio: 4.5×

Expected sensitivity ratio: √4.5 ≈ 2.1

Actual ratio: 201%/166% = 1.21

Hmm, less than expected. Why?

Because Leland formula COMPENSATES via A:
  A = (k/σ) × √(8/πΔt)

As dt increases, A decreases (less compensation needed for lower frequency).
This helps, but doesn't fully eliminate sensitivity.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION:
----------------------

For high volatility stocks:
  ⚠️ Rebalance MORE frequently
  ⚠️ Monthly rebalancing gives poor results (std ≈ 4.6)
  ✓ Daily or 2-day optimal (std ≈ 1.5-2.0)

For low volatility stocks:
  ✓ Can rebalance LESS frequently
  ✓ Monthly rebalancing acceptable (std ≈ 2.3)
  ✓ More flexibility in rebalancing schedule

TRANSACTION COST TRADEOFF:
--------------------------

More frequent rebalancing:
  ✓ Lower tracking error
  ✗ Higher transaction costs

Optimal frequency depends on k/σ ratio:
  - High k/σ (low vol): rebalance less (TC dominates)
  - Low k/σ (high vol): rebalance more (tracking error dominates)

TSLA optimal: daily (k/σ small, can afford frequent rebalancing)
AAPL optimal: 2-day or weekly (k/σ large, TC more important)

7. THEORETICAL EXPLANATION
================================================================================

WHY DOES VOLATILITY REGIME MATTER?
-----------------------------------

Leland formula: A = (k/σ) × √(8/πΔt)

Three regimes to consider:

1. HIGH VOLATILITY (σ >> k)
   - k/σ small → A small
   - Hedging error dominated by σΔt term
   - Transaction costs relatively unimportant
   - Formula adjustment small (~10-30%)

2. MODERATE VOLATILITY (σ ~ k)
   - k/σ moderate → A moderate
   - Hedging error and TC both matter
   - Formula adjustment moderate (~30-100%)

3. LOW VOLATILITY (σ << k)
   - k/σ large → A large
   - Transaction costs dominate
   - Formula adjustment large (>100%)

Our test cases:
  TSLA: σ=0.588, k=0.01 → k/σ=0.017 (regime 1)
  AAPL: σ=0.278, k=0.01 → k/σ=0.036 (regime 1-2 boundary)

OPTIMIZATION DIFFICULTY:
------------------------

Objective function: f(A) = Var(hedging error with A)

Shape depends on regime:

High vol (TSLA):
  - Clear minimum at A ≈ 0.43
  - Steep sides (bad A gives much worse result)
  - Easy to identify optimum
  - But also easy to overfit (steep gradient)

Low vol (AAPL):
  - Flatter minimum at A ≈ 0.91
  - Gentle sides (bad A gives similar result)
  - Hard to identify exact optimum
  - Less overfitting (flat gradient)

Paradox: Low vol is easier to optimize (flatter) but results matter less
        (tiny performance differences).

High vol is harder to optimize (steeper) but results matter more
(large performance differences).

So optimization noise:
  - High vol: Large absolute error, but small relative to signal
  - Low vol: Small absolute error, but large relative to signal

This explains why ML/Opt more competitive at low vol!

8. PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

FOR HIGH VOLATILITY STOCKS (σ > 0.5):
--------------------------------------

Hedging strategy:
  ✓ Use Classical Leland formula (dominates all alternatives)
  ✓ Rebalance frequently (daily or 2-day)
  ✓ Transaction costs less critical (k/σ small)

Parameter estimation:
  ✓ Focus on accurate σ estimation (drives performance)
  ✓ k estimation less critical (formula robust)
  ✓ Can use implied vol or historical vol

Risk management:
  ✓ Expect large hedging errors (std ≈ 1.5-4.5)
  ✓ Scale position sizes accordingly
  ✓ Monitor realized vol vs expected

FOR LOW VOLATILITY STOCKS (σ < 0.3):
-------------------------------------

Hedging strategy:
  ✓ Use Classical Leland formula (still best!)
  ✓ Can rebalance less frequently (2-day or weekly)
  ✓ Transaction costs more critical (k/σ large)

Parameter estimation:
  ⚠️ Accurate k estimation CRITICAL (performance sensitive)
  ⚠️ Accurate σ estimation still important
  ⚠️ Consider bid-ask spread in k

Risk management:
  ✓ Expect smaller hedging errors (std ≈ 0.7-2.3)
  ✓ But more sensitive to parameter errors
  ✓ Test robustness to k, σ misspecification

FOR MODERATE VOLATILITY STOCKS (0.3 < σ < 0.5):
------------------------------------------------

Hedging strategy:
  ✓ Use Classical Leland formula
  ✓ Rebalance daily or 2-day
  ✓ Balance TC and tracking error

Parameter estimation:
  ✓ Both σ and k estimation important
  ✓ Use reliable vol estimators (EWMA, GARCH)
  ✓ Monitor actual k (execution costs)

Risk management:
  ✓ Moderate hedging errors (between high and low vol)
  ✓ Regular performance monitoring
  ✓ Adjust rebalancing frequency based on realized TC

GENERAL RULES:
--------------

1. Classical Leland dominates in ALL volatility regimes
   → Always use it

2. Low vol more sensitive to k
   → Estimate k carefully for low vol stocks

3. High vol more sensitive to dt
   → Rebalance frequently for high vol stocks

4. Optimization methods don't help
   → Save time, use formula directly

5. Test on real data
   → Backtesting essential for validation
